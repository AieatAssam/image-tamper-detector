# Round 8 — additional detection mechanisms, and a scientific reference

Seven rounds established an honestly-measured baseline: fused held-out AUC
0.6775 over 168 rows, best single detector `double_jpeg` at 0.738. Adding ZERO
and Noisesniffer did not move fusion. The question this round answers is whether
the classical toolbox is genuinely exhausted or whether we have simply not
implemented the strongest members of it.

It is the latter. Three mechanisms are missing, and one of them is repeatedly
identified in the literature as the best training-free splicing localiser.

---

## R1 — Splicebuster (HIGH PRIORITY — the strongest classical method we lack)

Cozzolino, Poggi, Verdoliva, "Splicebuster: A new blind image splicing
detector", IEEE WIFS 2015. DOI 10.1109/WIFS.2015.7368565

Independent large-scale evaluations repeatedly place it at or near the top among
training-free methods; a 2026 comparison found it delivering the best F1 and IoU
among classical detectors. We have nothing of this class.

**Principle.** A camera's processing chain leaves a characteristic statistical
signature in the high-frequency residual. A spliced region came from a different
chain, so its residual statistics differ from the host's — even when no
compression or noise cue survives. Splicebuster does not need to know what
either camera was: it learns both distributions FROM THE IMAGE ITSELF and
segments the image into the two populations.

**Algorithm.**
1. High-pass residual: apply a third-order derivative filter along rows and
   columns, e.g. `r[i] = x[i-1] - 3x[i] + 3x[i+1] - x[i+2]`.
2. Quantise and truncate: `q = clip(round(r / q_step), -T, +T)` with T = 1 or 2.
   Small alphabet is the point — it keeps the histogram dense.
3. Co-occurrence: for each pixel accumulate the joint histogram of 4 consecutive
   quantised residuals along a direction. With T=1 that is 3^4 = 81 bins per
   direction; pool horizontal and vertical by symmetry.
4. Compute those features on overlapping blocks (e.g. 128x128, stride 8-32).
5. Fit a two-component model over the block features with EM — one component is
   the host, the other the splice — and take the posterior as the tamper map.
   Mahalanobis distance to a single fitted Gaussian is the cheaper variant the
   paper also describes; implement that first, it is far simpler and nearly as
   good in their own results.
6. Global score: the separation between the two components, or the maximum
   Mahalanobis distance over blocks.

**Implementation notes.** Pure numpy plus cv2. No training, no model download.
The co-occurrence step is the expensive part; vectorise it with strides rather
than looping. Budget it against the S06/S07 caps and downscale if needed.

**License.** GRIP-UNINA's own code is not permissively licensed — treat it as
paper-only, exactly as with ZERO and the CFA mosaic method in round 7. The
algorithm above is fully specified in the paper; implement independently.

**Why it should help here.** It is orthogonal to everything we have.
`double_jpeg`, `zero`, `ghosts` and `qtable` all read compression history;
`copy_move` reads geometry; `prnu`/Noisesniffer reads noise level. Splicebuster
reads the *statistical fingerprint of the processing chain*, which survives when
the others fail.

## R2 — Resampling detection (Popescu-Farid / Kirchner)

Popescu, Farid, "Exponential Detection of Traces of Resampling", IEEE
Transactions on Signal Processing 53(2), 2005.
Kirchner, "Fast and reliable resampling detection by spectral analysis of fixed
linear predictor residue", ACM MM&Sec 2008.

**Principle.** Resizing or rotating an image interpolates new pixels from old
ones, so each interpolated pixel is a deterministic linear combination of its
neighbours. That imposes a PERIODIC correlation structure whose period is set by
the resampling factor. Original camera output has no such structure. A pasted
region that was scaled or rotated to fit therefore carries a periodic signature
its host does not.

**Algorithm (use Kirchner's variant, not the original EM).**
1. Apply a FIXED linear predictor to the image and take the residual — Kirchner
   showed the expensive EM step of the original method is unnecessary, since the
   periodicity appears with a fixed filter too. This matters: EM per block would
   blow the runtime budget.
2. Take `|residual|` (the nonlinearity is essential — without it the periodicity
   does not surface).
3. Compute the 2-D DFT of that map and look for peaks at the frequencies
   resampling would produce.
4. Score by peak-to-background ratio; for localisation, do this per block and
   emit the map.

**Why it matters here.** Directly complements the existing set. Our corpus has a
`resize_then_save` family that is currently a pure false-positive trap; this
detector should be the one that legitimately fires on geometric resampling while
`cfa` correctly abstains. It also catches the common splice workflow where a
pasted object is scaled to fit — which `copy_move` misses because the match is
cross-image, not internal.

**Caution.** Global resampling is not tampering. A user's holiday photo resized
for the web is fully authentic. This detector must report LOCAL resampling
inconsistency, not "this image was resized". Getting that wrong turns it into a
false-positive machine. Score the DISAGREEMENT between blocks, not the presence
of resampling.

## R3 — AEROBLADE-style reconstruction error (OPT-IN, latent-diffusion only)

Ricker, Lukovnikov, Fischer, "AEROBLADE: Training-Free Detection of Latent
Diffusion Images Using Autoencoder Reconstruction Error", CVPR 2024.
arXiv:2401.17879

**Principle.** A latent diffusion model generates by decoding from a VAE latent
space. Its output therefore lies close to the autoencoder's reconstruction
manifold. Passing an image through that same autoencoder and measuring the
reconstruction error separates them: generated images reconstruct almost
perfectly, real photographs do not. Training-free — the autoencoder is a
component of the generator, not a classifier trained on examples.

Reported mean AP 0.992 across Stable Diffusion 1.1/1.5/2.1, Kandinsky 2.1 and
Midjourney, and notably robust to JPEG compression, cropping, blur and noise —
the perturbations that destroy most of our classical detectors.

**Available under a compatible license.** `madebyollin/taesd` is **MIT** — a
tiny distilled autoencoder compatible with Stable Diffusion's latent space,
about 1 MB. The AEROBLADE repository itself carries NO license, so it is
paper-only; the algorithm is simple enough that this is not a burden.

**Constraints that apply.**
- Decision D5 stands: NO torch. taesd ships safetensors only, so it needs a
  one-time ONNX export performed OUTSIDE this repo, exactly as S12 handles the
  learned detector. If that export cannot be produced, report BLOCKED — do not
  add torch.
- AEROBLADE measures reconstruction error with LPIPS, which needs a VGG/AlexNet
  backbone. A plain L1/L2 distance is weaker. If substituting, say so and report
  the measured cost rather than implying parity with the paper.
- taesd is a DISTILLED approximation of the SD autoencoder, not the exact one.
  The paper's headline numbers come from using the matching autoencoder.
  Expect lower performance and report it honestly.
- Scope: latent-diffusion images only. Useless against splicing, copy-move, or
  GAN output. Its `limitations` field must say so, like the S12 learned detector.

## R4 — DOCUMENTATION: a scientific reference for this repository

`plan/reference/detector-catalog.yaml` is agent-facing YAML. There is no
document a forensics researcher or a technically-minded user can read to
understand what this system measures and why.

Create `docs/detection-principles.md`. Per detector, in a consistent structure:

- **Principle** — the physical or statistical fact the detector exploits, in
  prose a domain reader can evaluate. Not "analyses compression artifacts" but
  what specifically about them, and why manipulation perturbs it.
- **Method** — the actual computation, with the formulas where they matter
  (the libjpeg quantisation scaling law, the generalised Benford model, the
  co-occurrence construction, the Hanley-McNeil standard error).
- **Citation** — full reference: authors, title, venue, year, DOI or arXiv id.
  Every detector must cite a primary source. Where an implementation was adapted
  rather than reimplemented, say which and under what license.
- **Signal direction** — what "more suspicious" means for the raw statistic.
- **Measured performance** — the current within-source AUC with its standard
  error and the corpus it was measured on. Include the ones that FAILED; a
  detector at 0.44 is a finding worth publishing, and hiding it would misrepresent
  the system.
- **Failure modes** — when it cannot speak, and when it lies. Every detector here
  has documented blind spots.
- **Provenance of the numbers** — the corpus, its size, its construction, and
  the fact that half of it is synthetic.

Also add:
- A short "what this system cannot do" section. A forensics tool that omits its
  limits invites exactly the overconfident use it should prevent.
- A bibliography section collecting every citation in one place, so the
  references survive independently of the per-detector entries.
- The licensing table from round 7: which methods were reimplemented from papers
  and which adapted from permissively-licensed code. This is a legal record, not
  a courtesy.

Keep `detector-catalog.yaml` as the machine-readable source of truth and have
the document reference it, rather than duplicating numbers that will drift.

## Measurement discipline (unchanged, and it matters more as detectors accumulate)

- Re-measure with `within_source_auc` and its Hanley-McNeil SE, before and after.
- A new detector that lands at chance is a result. ZERO did exactly that in
  round 7 and reporting it plainly was correct.
- Do NOT tune to raise the fused AUC.
- Do not let a below-chance detector hold positive weight; the guard already
  enforces this.
