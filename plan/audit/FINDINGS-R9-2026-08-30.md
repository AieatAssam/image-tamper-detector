# Round 9 — AI-generated image detection, and a coverage sweep

## The coverage matrix

Three families of tampering. Mapping our 16 detectors onto them exposes the gap.

| mechanism | detectors | best within-source AUC | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recompression / re-save** | double_jpeg, jpeg_ghosts, zero, qtable, ela | 0.660 (double_jpeg) | adequate |
| **Splicing** (paste from another image) | splicebuster, zero, ghosts, prnu, resampling | 0.669 synthetic / 0.487 real | weak, and does not generalise |
| **Copy-move** (paste from same image) | copy_move | 0.585 | weak but real |
| **Local retouch** | prnu, ela, splicebuster | ~0.54 | weak |
| **AI GENERATION** | spectral, entropy, cfa, learned, aeroblade | **0.659 (learned, face-only)** | **barely covered** |
| **Provenance** | c2pa, exif | n/a — declarative | correct but rarely present |

AI generation is the weakest column and the one that matters most now.
Everything in it is compromised:

- `spectral` 0.536 — a 2019-era GAN upsampling assumption; modern diffusion
  decoders leave much weaker spectral peaks.
- `entropy` 0.473 — below chance, unreviewed blog heuristic.
- `cfa` — zero applicable rows anywhere.
- `learned` 0.659, weight 0.120 — the only one working, and it is a FACE
  deepfake ViT. Useless on landscapes, documents, or receipts.
- `aeroblade` — implemented but inert; the ONNX weights were never fetched.

## THE PRIMARY BLOCKER — we cannot measure AI detection at all

`data/corpus/MANIFEST.yaml`: **12 real_ai images against 400 IMD2020 rows.**
Worse, most carry no recorded generator:

    Midjourney v4: 1, Stable Diffusion 3.5: 1, xAI Aurora: 1, unattributed: 9

Twelve images cannot measure anything, and they are outnumbered 33:1 by
classical manipulations, so any AI detector is diluted to noise in the pooled
fit. This is the same structural problem the project hit in round 3 with source
identity and in round 8 with splicebuster's scope. Fix the corpus FIRST; adding
detectors before that just adds unmeasurable code.

### K1 — ELSA_D3 / ELSA1M (CC-BY-4.0, not gated, parquet)

`elsaEU/ELSA1M_track1` — verified 2026-08-30: license cc-by-4.0, `gated: false`,
892 parquet shards, size category 10K-100K.

Schema carries exactly what we need:
    image             the generated image
    model             which generator produced it
    url_real_image    URL of the REAL counterpart for the same prompt

That last field is the important one. It gives real/generated PAIRS, which
satisfies `source_balance_rule` natively — the same structural property that
made IMD2020 usable in round 6b. Parquet means no giant archive download; sample
shards and stream.

### K2 — GenImage (Apache-2.0, not gated)

`ENSTA-U2IS/GenImage` — verified: apache-2.0, `gated: false`, 209 files, split
zip archives per generator (ADM and others). GenImage is the standard
million-scale benchmark across 8 generators. Heavier to fetch than ELSA; treat
as the second source, and only if ELSA does not yield enough generator
diversity.

Target: at least **150 AI images across at least 6 distinct generators**, paired
with their real counterparts. Record the generator per image from the dataset's
own `model` field — do not infer it. Same D7b licensing rule as IMD2020: bytes
into a gitignored directory, manifest and citation committed, never the images.

## New detectors

### K3 — NPR, Neighboring Pixel Relationships (HIGH PRIORITY)

Tan et al., "Rethinking the Up-Sampling Operations in CNN-based Generative
Network for Generalizable Deepfake Detection", CVPR 2024, arXiv:2312.10461.

Reported +11.6% mean accuracy over prior work across **28 distinct GAN and
diffusion models**, trained only on ProGAN — the strongest generalisation result
in this literature.

**The feature is trivial arithmetic.** Divide the image into 2x2 patches (2x2
is the paper's default and it reports 2x2 beating 3x3). Within each patch,
subtract a reference element from every element:

    v_hat = { w_1 - w_j , ... , w_n - w_j }

That relative-difference map IS the NPR representation. Upsampling operators
make neighbouring pixels locally interdependent, so in generated images those
intra-patch differences are structured and degenerate; in camera images they
follow natural image statistics.

**Licensing / weights.** The repository (chuangchuangtan/NPR-DeepfakeDetection)
declares NO license, so it is paper-only under the round-7 rule. Its classifier
is a 1.44M-parameter CNN trained on ProGAN — we cannot use those weights and
D5 forbids torch anyway.

**So implement the TRAINING-FREE statistic, not the classifier.** The NPR map
itself has measurable properties: the ratio of intra-patch variance to
inter-patch variance, the fraction of 2x2 patches that are near-constant, and
the entropy of the difference distribution. Real photographs and upsampled
generations differ on these without any learned model. State clearly in the
docs that this is a training-free statistic derived from the paper's
representation, NOT a reproduction of the paper's detector, and that its
performance should not be compared to the paper's headline numbers.

### K4 — Activate AEROBLADE

It is implemented and inert because the TAESD ONNX weights were never produced.
With a real AI corpus from K1 it becomes measurable for the first time, and it
is the method specifically designed for LATENT DIFFUSION, which is what most
current generators are. Produce the ONNX export outside this repo per the S12
pattern, or report BLOCKED — but do not leave it inert without saying why.

### K5 — Replace `entropy` with MLEP, or delete it

Our `entropy` detector is at 0.473, below chance, from an unreviewed blog post.
There is a peer-reviewed successor in the same family: "MLEP: Multi-granularity
Local Entropy Patterns for Generalized AI-generated Image Detection",
arXiv:2504.13726, which computes local entropy patterns at multiple granularities
rather than the single-radius cross-channel matching we implement.

Measure MLEP against the K1 corpus. If it beats the current entropy detector,
replace it. If neither works, DELETE `entropy` — round 7 deferred that decision
pending evidence, and this round supplies the evidence. A below-chance detector
carried across nine rounds is technical debt, not caution.

### K6 — CLIP-feature detector (evaluate, may end BLOCKED)

Ojha, Li, Lee, "Towards Universal Fake Image Detection", CVPR 2023, and the
follow-on literature, establish that a linear probe on frozen CLIP ViT-L/14
features generalises across generators far better than any hand-crafted feature.
It is the dominant approach in the 2026 NTIRE challenge results.

This needs an ONNX CLIP image encoder and a small trained probe. The encoder is
available in ONNX under permissive licenses; the probe would have to be fitted
on the K1 corpus, which is legitimate — it is a linear model on frozen features,
fitted with our own calibration machinery.

Evaluate feasibility. If it needs torch, it is BLOCKED under D5. Report either
way; do not add torch.

## Constraints (unchanged, and they bind harder here)

- D5: NEVER torch. ONNX runtime only, opt-in, degrading to NOT_APPLICABLE.
- D7b: no image bytes committed; no source whose licence you cannot establish.
- Paper-only for anything without a permissive license: NPR, AEROBLADE, TruFor.
- Every new detector reports NOT_APPLICABLE where its assumptions fail, checked
  from the IMAGE at inference — the round-8c lesson. A detector that only works
  on latent-diffusion output must say so per image, not per corpus.
- Do NOT tune to raise the fused AUC. If AI detectors remain weak on a proper
  corpus, that is the finding, and it is a more important finding than any of
  the classical work in rounds 7-8.
