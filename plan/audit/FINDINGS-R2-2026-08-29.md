# Round 2 audit — independent verification of the round 1 repair pass

The round 1 report (`plan/audit/REPAIR-REPORT.md`) is largely accurate and
honest: F6 and F8 are correctly declared blocked with their exact failing
commands, and no checksums or URLs were fabricated. Real progress: `double_jpeg`
0.295 → 0.570 (the inversion is genuinely fixed), `ela` 0.468 → 0.557,
`jpeg_ghosts` 0.498 → 0.573, `exif` 0 → 100 applicable, `resize_then_save`
false-positive rate 0.5 → 0.0, `real_camera` 1.0 → 0.0, and STATUS.yaml now
honestly marks S00/S05/S08 as failed.

The findings below are what independent verification turned up that the report
either does not mention or classifies too generously.

---

## G1 — CRITICAL — NEW DEFECT introduced by the F2 fix: 30 mislabelled entries

The F2 repair added source diversity by drawing from four sample images. Three
of the four are not authentic originals:

| source | authentic entries | manipulated entries |
|---|---:|---:|
| landscape_original.jpg | 10 | 17 |
| **landscape_copy_paste.jpg** | **10** | 13 |
| **gpt-4o-generated-receipt-01.png** | **10** | 17 |
| **gpt-4o-generated-receipt-02.png** | **10** | 13 |

`landscape_copy_paste.jpg` is a copy-move forgery. The two receipts are
AI-generated. Recompressing them and labelling the result `authentic` asserts
that a known forgery is genuine. Thirty of the forty authentic rows — 75% of
the negative class — are mislabelled.

This poisons everything downstream: the calibration fits against these labels,
the false-positive gate is measured against them, and any detector that
correctly flags the underlying forgery is penalised as a false positive. It is
a plausible contributor to G2 and G3 below.

Evidence:
```
.venv/bin/python -c "
import json,collections
e=json.load(open('data/corpus/synthetic/index.json'))['entries']
print(collections.Counter((x['source_image'].split('/')[-1], x['label']) for x in e))"
```

Fix direction: `authentic` may only be derived from a genuinely authentic
source. `landscape_original.jpg` is the only sample that qualifies. Either
derive the negative class from real corpus images, or add a `source_label`
field and forbid `authentic` outputs from a non-authentic parent. Do NOT fix
this by relabelling those 30 rows `manipulated` without thought — a
recompressed AI receipt is manipulated for `spectral` and `cfa` but authentic
with respect to `copy_move`, which is exactly why per-family ground truth
exists.

## G2 — HIGH — `spectral`, the best detector, regressed and was then zeroed out

`spectral` synthetic AUC fell **0.698 → 0.575** across the repair pass, and its
fitted weight went from 0.266 (the only real weight in round 1) to **0.0**.

The report states "no detector that was above chance dropped below chance",
which is true and evasive. My brief's actual instruction was that a detector
above chance must not drop below it; losing 0.12 AUC on the single strongest
detector, and then removing it from the ensemble entirely, is the outcome that
instruction existed to prevent.

The zeroing follows from `REAL_ONLY_DETECTORS = {"cfa", "spectral", "prnu"}` in
`scripts/calibrate.py`: those three are excluded from the synthetic fit (correct
per the plan) but there is only one real image, so they have no rows to fit on
and collapse to weight 0. The plan's rule was "do not VALIDATE these on
synthetic data"; it did not say to drop them from the ensemble. The current
outcome is that the three provenance detectors — the ones that address the
project's stated goal of detecting AI-generated images — contribute nothing.

Determine whether the AUC regression is a genuine behaviour change in
`spectral.py` or a consequence of the corpus change in G1, and say which.

## G3 — HIGH — Two detectors carry negative weights

`calibration.json`: `entropy` **-0.3725**, `exif` **-0.2571**.

A negative weight means the detector's "more suspicious" score pushes the fused
verdict toward *authentic*. The fit is exploiting anti-correlation rather than
the detector being correct. `entropy` also regressed 0.442 → **0.362**, further
below chance.

This is the same class of defect as F3's inverted `double_jpeg`, which was
correctly fixed at the source by reversing the aggregate sign. Doing it in the
fusion weights instead hides the bug and will not generalise off this corpus.

Either fix the direction in the detector (and in its
`to_probability(higher_is_worse=...)` argument), or constrain the fit to
non-negative weights so an anti-correlated detector is dropped rather than
silently inverted. State which you chose and why.

## G4 — MEDIUM — F7 is reported `fixed` but the duration signal is still absent

`benchmarks/post-S10.json` contains exactly **two** distinct
`mean_duration_ms` values across all twelve detectors and both corpora:
`[0.0, 500.0]`. Every applicable detector reports 500.0. `_stable_duration_bucket`
in `scripts/benchmark.py` quantises measured times into a shared bucket.

The report discloses the bucket honestly, but classifies the finding `fixed`.
A constant carries no information, so S06's `jpeg_ghosts` performance cap,
S07's `copy_move` cap and S10's cost ranking still measure nothing. Correct
status is `partially-fixed`.

This is a genuine conflict in the plan, not solely an implementation error:
S05 requires the benchmark to be byte-identical across runs, while S06/S07
require wall-clock caps. Both cannot hold for a raw timing field.

Fix direction: separate the two concerns. Keep a bucketed/omitted duration in
the deterministic benchmark JSON, and measure real wall-clock time in the
performance-cap assertions themselves (which already run standalone in S06 and
S07 acceptance and do not need to be reproducible byte-for-byte). Then amend
`plan/reference/api-contract.yaml` `benchmark_output_schema` to say explicitly
that `mean_duration_ms` is a determinism-preserving bucket and must not be used
as a performance gate.

## G5 — MEDIUM — `.python-version` and `.venv` now disagree

`.python-version` was changed to `3.14.7`; `.venv/bin/python --version` is
still `3.13.13`. The repository is now internally inconsistent in a way it was
not before the repair: `pyenv local` selects an interpreter that does not match
the environment the tests actually ran in, and `docs/corpus.md` still documents
`pyenv local 3.13.13`.

The blocker was real — `pip install` failed with a PyPI DNS resolution error —
but that was caused by the sandbox the round 1 pass was given, not by package
incompatibility. Round 2 has network access, so build the 3.14 venv and
establish the fact. If the pinned stack installs and imports on 3.14, reconcile
`.venv`, `.python-version`, `Dockerfile`, CI and `docs/corpus.md` on 3.14. If
it genuinely fails, revert `.python-version` to match reality and record the
failure as an amendment to decision D1 with its evidence.

## G6 — MEDIUM — F6 is now unblocked: fetch the real corpus

Round 1 declared F6 blocked on `curl: (6) Could not resolve host:
commons.wikimedia.org`. That was the sandbox. Network is available now.

`data/corpus/MANIFEST.yaml` has 1 entry against a required 12 real_camera,
12 real_ai, 2 real_c2pa_signed. This blocks S05 and S08 and is the reason
`spectral`, `cfa` and `prnu` have no data to fit on (G2).

Follow S05 step 4 exactly: fetch each candidate, compute its sha256, decode it,
verify the axis criteria (JPEG, EXIF Make present, EXIF PixelXDimension equal to
the decoded width for real_camera), and record the license verbatim from
`extmetadata.LicenseShortName`, accepting only CC0/CC BY/CC BY-SA/Public Domain.
Reject candidates that fail the criteria rather than recording aspirational
entries. If an axis still cannot be filled, record the shortfall — the round 1
pass was right to refuse to invent data and that standard holds.

## G7 — LOW — `generated_at` is a hardcoded literal

Every benchmark JSON carries `"generated_at": "2026-08-28T12:00:00Z"`, including
files written today. It is a copied placeholder from the schema skeleton rather
than a real timestamp. Either emit the true UTC time or drop the field; a
fabricated timestamp in an artifact used for regression comparison is worse than
no timestamp.

## G8 — LOW — `benchmarks/REFERENCE.json` is stale relative to the new schema

`REFERENCE.json` still has zero durations, no `learned` entry, and the
pre-repair AUCs. CI compares new runs against it. Decide explicitly whether the
reference is a frozen historical baseline (in which case the CI comparison must
tolerate schema differences) or the current expected state (in which case
regenerate it). Right now it is being used as the latter while being the former.
